# Calendar examples from the diary of Abraham De la Pryme

Evidence for the examples in the topics on old-style dates. This is a narrow exception to the rule that examples come from the Brontë dataset (DEC-202). It is not a second dataset: it holds six claims, each with its evidence, and nothing else.

Part of the [decision log](../../../authoring/decision-log.md). Written 5 November 2026.

## 1. Why a second source

Every date in the Brontë dataset falls after 1752, so it is all Gregorian. The Julian calendar, a year that began on 25 March and a year written with two numbers need a source from before the change. Abraham De la Pryme (1671 to 1704) was a Yorkshire diarist and antiquary. His diary was printed in 1870, and the printed edition is on the Internet Archive.

## 3. The claims

All notes are in our own words. Nothing is copied from the edition, and the examples in the topics use dates and facts only.

| Id | Claim | Evidence | Page | Used for |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Abraham was born on 15 January 1671, in his own words. | The opening of the diary. | 1 | The worked example. |
| P2 | His family list, in his own writing, gives the same birth day and year. | The family notices at the start of the diary, printed in the appendix. | 258 | The worked example. |
| P3 | His parents married on 3 April 1670. | The same family list. | 258 | The worked example. |
| P4 | His brother Peter was born on 29 April 1672. | The same family list. | 258 | The worked example. |
| P5 | The editor prints the year of a degree at St John's College, Cambridge, as 1692-3 for a man who took it in the early months of what we now call 1693. | An editor's footnote about a St John's man. | 40 | The old-style year. |
| P6 | The preface says Abraham took his own degree in January 1693. | The preface. | xviii | The old-style year. |

## 4. What the claims show

- **P1 to P4.** If Abraham's year began on 25 March, January 1671 would be January 1672 by our count. His brother would then be born about three months later, which cannot be. So he began his year on 1 January, and his birth year is 1671 whichever way it is counted. Before 25 March, a writer might use either year: that is why the old-style year matters.
- **P5 and P6.** The same early months of the year appear as 1693 in one place and as 1692-3 in another. They name the same year in two ways.
- **A library catalogue.** A catalogue may give a different birth year, by adding a year for the old-style rule. We have not read one, so no catalogue is a claim here.

The example does not settle any scholarly debate. It shows how the same year can be written in more than one way.

## 5. Privacy check

Everyone named in the topics was born in the 17th century. Nobody could be living. The topics name Abraham, his parents and his brother only.
