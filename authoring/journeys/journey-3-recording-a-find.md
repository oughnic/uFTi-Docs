# Journey 3: Recording a find

> "I have a certificate. Where does it go?"

Part of [WP0](README.md). Status: draft for testing, 16 October 2026. The format is from the [content model, section 4](../content-model.md#4-user-journeys). Screen names and labels are provisional until WP4 confirms them against the app.

| Field | |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | A birth certificate arrived in the post. Or a marriage certificate turned up in a drawer after a relative died. It is the first real document they have held. |
| **Person** | Newcomer with a small tree already. They do not know the words source, citation or repository. They want to "do it properly" but not to type everything. |
| **Devices** | Android phone, with the certificate on the table. Or Windows, with a scanner or a folder of scans. |
| **Success** | The fact shows on the person's page with the certificate as its source. They can see where the certificate is kept, and a picture of it. |
| **Session task** | "Here is a card. It says Charlotte Brontë married Arthur Bell Nicholls. Record this on Charlotte's page, and say the card is where you found it." Once WP4b has the evidence register, the card gets its date and place from there. |

## Steps

| # | What they try, in their words | What they ask, and what would stop them | Content that answers it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Find the person it is about." | "Which one is it? There are two Marias." The sample has a mother and a daughter called Maria. | Task: Find a person. Screen: the people list shows dates to tell people apart. |
| 2 | "Put in what the certificate says." | "Do I type everything on it?" "Is a birth an event or a fact?" "The place has a different name now." | Task: Add an event. Task: Add a place. Reference: event types. Guide: places and boundaries that change. |
| 3 | "Say it came from this certificate." | "Source or citation? Which do I fill in first?" "What goes in which box?" | Quick Start page 5. Task: Add a source to a fact. Cue: record where you found it. Glossary: source, citation. |
| 4 | "Say where the certificate is." | "Repository? It is in my drawer." | Task: Add a repository. Glossary: repository, in plain words: an archive, or where you keep a thing. |
| 5 | "Keep a picture of it with the person." | "Does the picture go on the person or on the source?" | Task: Add a photo of a document. Concept: photos, sources and people. |
| 6 | "Note the bits that do not match what Mum told me." | "Where do I write that?" | Task: Add a research note. This leads to [Journey 4](journey-4-checking-a-doubt.md). |

## On an Android phone

- Step 5 uses the camera. The app asks for permission the first time. To confirm.
- The certificate is large. A photo of the whole page is hard to read on a phone. The task says to photograph it in good light, flat, and to add the back if it has writing.

## On Windows

- Step 5 uses a scan or a file. The file picker opens.
- A scanner gives a better picture than a phone. The task says so and leaves scanning to the scanner's own instructions.

## On a tablet or an iPhone

- Tablet: the camera as the phone. The wider screen shows the person and the new fact together.
- iPhone and iPad: the camera comes through the system picker. The iOS Simulator has no camera, so that screen has no screenshot and is described in words.

## What this journey decides

- **Quick Start.** Page 5, "Say where you found it", is this journey in miniature. Its what-next link is "Record what you have found", shared with [Journey 7](journey-7-getting-a-photo-in.md).
- **Task finder.** "Record a birth, marriage or death", "say where I found something", "record where a document is kept", "add a photo".
- **Help targets.** Add or edit an event, the date editor, the place picker, add or edit a source, add a source to a fact, add or edit a repository, add a photo.
- **Topics first.** Add an event. Add a source to a fact. Add a repository. Add a photo of a document.
- **For the app team (tier 0).** The words *source*, *citation* and *repository* each need a one-line hint on screen. The order on screen should match the order a person thinks in: the fact, then where it came from, then where that is kept.
