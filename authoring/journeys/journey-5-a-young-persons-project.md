# Journey 5: A young person's project

> "It is for school. Who do I put in, and what can I show?"

Part of [WP0](README.md). Status: draft for testing, 16 October 2026. The format is from the [content model, section 4](../content-model.md#4-user-journeys). Screen names and labels are provisional until WP4 confirms them against the app.

| Field | |
|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Homework: "make your family tree" over half term. Or a grandparent's big birthday, and they want to make something to give. |
| **Person** | Young person, 11 to 14. Reads at the manual's target level. Most of the people in the tree are alive. They have to ask relatives for dates. They worry: "Am I allowed to put Nan's birthday in?" and "What do I hand in?" |
| **Devices** | Android phone. Or the family's Windows laptop. At school, often an iPad. |
| **Success** | A tree with their family and some photos, which they are allowed to show. They asked everyone who is alive. They have something to show in class. |
| **Session task** | "Imagine this is for school. Start a tree for a made-up family of six, with two sets of parents for one child. Add this picture to someone. Then tell me what you would check before showing it in class." The picture is a public-domain image from the dataset's media list. |

## Steps

| # | What they try, in their words | What they ask, and what would stop them | Content that answers it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "See what a finished one looks like." | "Who are these Brontë people?" | Quick Start page 3. Concept: about the sample. |
| 2 | "Start my own tree." | "Can I call it anything?" "It asks who made it. Me?" | Quick Start page 4. Task: Create a workspace. |
| 3 | "Put in me, my brother, Mum and Dad." | "Mum and Dad are not together. Do I need two families?" "Can I add my step-dad and my dad?" | Task: Add a person. Task: Add a second family for a person. Concept: families of all shapes. |
| 4 | "Ask Nan about her parents and add them." | "She is not sure of the year." "Do I write down what she said?" | Task: Enter a date you are not sure of. Task: Add a note. |
| 5 | "Put in a photo of Nan." | "Do I need to ask her first?" | Task: Add a photo. Guide: ask before you add a living person's picture, written for young readers. |
| 6 | "Show it in class." | "How do I get it on the whiteboard?" "Should I take out the birthdays?" | Task: View the family tree. Task: Share a picture of your tree, if the app offers one (Q28). Checklist: living people and what to share. |

## On an Android phone

- Their own phone, so the camera is to hand for step 5.
- Showing the tree in class means the phone's own screenshot, unless the app shares a picture (Q28).

## On Windows

- A shared family laptop. The workspace may sit next to a parent's. The topic on workspaces says each tree is its own workspace.
- Step 6: a screenshot or the app's own share, then a slide or a print from another program.

## On a tablet or an iPhone

- iPad: the likely school device. Landscape, with the tree and the person side by side. Pictures must show this.
- iPhone and tablet: the camera as the phone.

## What this journey decides

- **Quick Start.** Pages 3 and 4 are written so an 11-year-old can follow them alone. The sessions must include at least one young person. Its what-next link is "Build your first tree", shared with [Journey 1](journey-1-starting-from-nothing.md).
- **Task finder.** "Add a person", "add a second family", "add a photo", "keep living people private when I share", "show my tree to someone".
- **Help targets.** Add or edit a family, add a photo, the tree.
- **Topics first.** Add a second family for a person. The guide's living-people checklist, written for young readers first, because it is the plainest version.
- **For the app team (tier 0).** A way to share a picture of the tree would complete this journey (Q28). Family forms must not assume two parents who are together.
