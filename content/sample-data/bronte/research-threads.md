# Research threads

Five threads for stage 7, each with its question, the evidence for and against, how each piece was weighed, and a conclusion with its confidence or the thread left open. After them: the smaller discrepancies we met and concluded in the register, and the searches that found nothing.

**Confidence words.** *High*: independent evidence agrees and nothing credible contradicts it. *Moderate*: the better evidence points one way, but a credible source disagrees or an original has not been seen. *Low*: a single weak source. *Open*: we do not conclude.

**Weighing rules we used.** An original record beats a transcription; a transcription beats an index; a record made at the time beats a memory; a work that cites its records beats one that does not. Two records that share one informant are one piece of evidence, not two. A date of baptism is a ceiling on a date of birth, not the date itself. An age on a memorial depends on who gave it.

## T01. What was Patrick's surname at birth, and when did he become Brontë?

**Why it matters.** Searching for his family in Irish records under *Brontë* will fail. The name he was born with has at least three spellings in the records.

**Evidence**

| # | Source | What it says | Kind | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | S05, Cambridge alumni database | Son of "Hugh [Prunty], farmer"; "In the parish Register the name occurs as Brunty" | Compiled reference, citing the register | Strong for the register spelling |
| 2 | S06, DNB 1886 | "one of the ten children of Hugh Prunty or Brontë. He changed his paternal name to Brontë shortly before leaving Ireland" | Authored, secondary | Moderate; no record cited for the timing |
| 3 | S07, Shorter 1896 | The St John's admission list has "Patrick Branty", "written in another handwriting". The register of his birth has "Brunty" and "Bruntee". An earlier researcher found Brunty, Bruntee, Bronty and Branty, "but never in Patrick Bronte's handwriting". "His signature through his successive curacies is Bronte." Shorter's own theory about Nelson's title he calls one for which "there is, of course, not one scrap of evidence" | Authored, secondary, reporting records seen | Strong for the spellings; the theory is set aside |
| 4 | S10, Leyland 1886 | "his father, Hugh Bronte, or Brunty"; "the name is spelled Brunty" | Authored, secondary | Supports Brunty |
| 5 | S01, Gaskell 1857 | Calls the father "Hugh Bronte" and gives no other spelling | Authored, secondary | Silence; not evidence against |

**Weighing.** Three independent authors and the alumni database report Brunty as the register spelling. Prunty is how two compiled references give the father's name. Branty is one clerk's spelling, seen by Shorter in the college list. On *when*: the DNB says before he left Ireland; Shorter adds that no signature survives from before 1799, and that the Cambridge list was written by someone else. Both agree he signed Brontë from his curacies onward.

**Conclusion.** His birth name is spelled *Brunty* in the parish register; *Prunty* is another spelling of the same family name; *Branty* is a clerk's spelling at Cambridge. He used *Brontë* by the time of his ordination in 1806 at the latest. Confidence: **high** for the spellings and for the change having happened by 1806. **Open:** exactly when he began to write Brontë, and why.

**Status in the register:** C002, sources disagree, concluded; the timing stays open.

**What the dataset records.** Name: Patrick Brontë. Variants: Brunty, Prunty, Branty, Bruntee, Bronty. Research note N11.

## T02. Where in County Down was Patrick born?

**Why it matters.** It shows a place recorded at different levels (townland, parish, county) by different sources, and a county that is now in a different country from the rest of the island.

**Evidence**

| # | Source | What it says | Kind | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | S05 | "of Emdale, Co. Down, Ireland. B. there Mar. 17, 1777"; the entry's addenda credit a 1991 communication from Dr J. R. V. Barker | Compiled reference, with a modern scholarly correction | Strong |
| 2 | S23 | "Patrick's birthplace at Emdale" | Tourism page | Weak but independent of the 19th-century chain |
| 3 | S06 | "born on 17 March 1777 at Ahaderg, co. Down" | Authored, 1886 | Moderate |
| 4 | S07 | "born at Ahaderg, County Down" | Authored, 1896 | Moderate; may follow S06 or S01 |
| 5 | S01 | "a native of the County Down"; his father "settled in the parish of Ahaderg, near Loughbrickland" | Authored, 1857; from Patrick himself | Strong for the county; says where the father settled, not where Patrick was born |
| 6 | S10 | "a native of the county Down ... at the house of his father, Hugh Bronte, or Brunty, at Ahaderg" | Authored, 1886 | Moderate |
| 7 | S24 | "born ... in Drumballyroney in County Down" | Newspaper, 2024 | Weak |

**Weighing.** Emdale is a townland name; Ahaderg and Drumballyroney are parish names. The sources may be naming the same place at different levels, or different places; none of them says. The chain Gaskell, DNB, Shorter, Leyland rests on Gaskell's sentence about where the father settled. The alumni database's Emdale carries a modern scholar's correction. We did not find a source that places Emdale in either parish, so we do not say which.

**Conclusion.** Born in County Down on 17 March 1777: **high** confidence, every source agrees. Townland Emdale: **moderate** confidence. Parish: **open**.

**Status in the register:** C001, sources disagree, concluded for the county and townland; open for the parish.

**What the dataset records.** Birth: 17 March 1777, Emdale, County Down, Ireland. Research note N12. The place hierarchy stops at the townland and the county.

## T03. How old were Branwell, Emily and Anne when they died?

**Why it matters.** Three memorials give three sets of ages, and the parish register agrees with the wrong one. It is the clearest example in the dataset of records that share an informant.

**Evidence**

| Person | First tablet (S03) | Burial register (S13) | Gravestone, Scarborough (S20) | 1858 tablet (S04) | Baptised (S06) | Age the baptism implies at death |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Branwell, died 24 September 1848 | aged 30 | 30y | | aged 31 | 23 July 1817 | 31 |
| Emily, died 19 December 1848 | aged 29 | 29y | | aged 30 | 20 August 1818 | 30 |
| Anne, died 28 May 1849 | aged 27 | not transcribed (buried at Scarborough) | 28 | aged 29 | 25 March 1820 | 29 |

Also: Gaskell's footnote says a reviewer pointed out the discrepancy in Anne's age and that she "did not think it of sufficient consequence to be rectified by an examination of the register of births" (S01). The museum gives 31, 30 and 29 (S18). Robinson gives Anne as twenty-nine (S11). The *Yorkshire Post* gives Anne's true age as 29 against the stone's 28 (S20).

**Weighing.** The first tablet and the burial register agree with each other for Branwell and Emily, and both are a year short. They were made within weeks of each other for the same household, so they count as one piece of evidence, not two. The baptism dates are records made at the time by a different hand and are the strongest evidence. The 1858 tablet matches the baptisms. Anne's stone has a third figure again; the *Yorkshire Post* account says Charlotte found several errors on it and corrected the others.

**Conclusion.** Branwell 31, Emily 30, Anne 29. Confidence: **high**.

**Status in the register:** C090, C109, C131, sources disagree, concluded.

**What the dataset records.** Each death event cites every source with the age it gives. The research note N13 holds the conclusion. The manual's "reconcile evidence" topic uses Anne's three ages.

## T04. In which year were Maria, Elizabeth and Anne born?

**Why it matters.** Published works differ by a year for each of them, and no source we opened gives a day of birth. It shows how to record a birth you cannot yet pin down.

**Evidence**

| Person | Shorter 1896 (S07) | Robinson 1883 (S11) | Baptism (S06) | Age on the tablets | What the age implies | Place |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Maria | 1813 | | not given | "in the 12th year of her age" at 6 May 1825 (S03, S04) | born between 7 May 1813 and 6 May 1814 | Hartshead (S01, S06, S07) |
| Elizabeth | 1814 | | 26 August 1815 | "in the 11th year of her age" at 15 June 1825 (S03, S04) | born between 16 June 1814 and 15 June 1815 | Hartshead (S01, S07) |
| Anne | 1819 | 1819 | 25 March 1820 | aged 29 at 28 May 1849 (S04) | born between 29 May 1819 and 28 May 1820 | Thornton (S01 footnote, S10) |

Also: Gaskell says Maria "could only have been a few months more than six years old" on 25 February 1820 (S01), which points to mid or late 1813. The DNB heads its article "Anne Brontë (1820-1849)" (S06). Leyland says Anne was born "probably just before" the move to Haworth on 25 February 1820 (S10). The museum says Maria died aged 11 and Elizabeth aged 10 (S18).

**Weighing.** A baptism date is only a ceiling. "In the 12th year" means aged 11, which gives a twelve-month window. The biographies give years without saying where they got them, and they disagree with each other for Anne (1819) against the DNB (1820). For Elizabeth, a baptism in August 1815 fits a birth in early 1815 or in late 1814. For Anne, the March 1820 baptism and Leyland's remark favour early 1820, but 1819 is not excluded by anything we opened.

**Conclusion.** **Open.** We record ranges: Maria 1813 to 1814, Elizabeth 1814 to 1815, Anne 1819 to 1820, each with its baptism date as an exact fact where we have one. Charlotte (21 April 1816), Branwell (1817) and Emily (1818) are not in doubt.

**What would close it.** The baptism entries themselves at West Yorkshire Archive Service (Hartshead for Maria, Thornton for the others), some of which may record the date of birth, or a scholarly biography that cites them.

**Status in the register:** C020, C025, C120, sources disagree, thread open.

**What the dataset records.** A date range for each birth, with the thread attached, and research note N14.

## T05. Why does the 1841 census give Patrick's age as 60?

**Why it matters.** The 1841 census disagrees with the baptism by design. A reader who does not know the rule will think one of them is wrong.

**Evidence**

| # | Source | What it says | Kind | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | S17 | On the 6 June 1841 return for the Parsonage, Patrick and Elizabeth Branwell are "both listed as being 60, but they were both 64"; Anne "had her age listed as 20 but then crossed out and written as 19" though she was 21; Emily and Martha Brown are also listed; Tabby was elsewhere | A biographer's reading of the return; the image not shown | Single source for the content |
| 2 | S16 | The 1841 census was taken on 6 June; ages were "rounded down to the nearest five years for those aged 15 or over" | The National Archives' guide | Strong for the rule |
| 3 | S15 | HO 107/1295 covers "Parish: Bradford (part); Township: Haworth" in 1841 | Catalogue | Strong for where the record is |
| 4 | S05, S06 | Born 17 March 1777, so 64 on 6 June 1841 | As in T02 | Strong |
| 5 | S14 | FreeCEN has no 1841 entry for Bronte | Negative search | The transcription is not available |

**Weighing.** The rule explains 60 exactly: 64 rounds down to 60. Anne's "20" altered to "19" shows the rule was not always applied, since a 21-year-old should have been written as 20. Everything about the return's content rests on one second-hand reading.

**Conclusion.** 60 is the age the census rules produced for a man of 64; it is not evidence against 1777. Confidence in the explanation: **high**. The return's content: **open** until the image is read.

**Status in the register:** C047, single source; thread open.

**What the dataset records.** A census event for Patrick on 6 June 1841 at Haworth, citing HO 107/1295, with the age "60" as the return gives it and the note that it is rounded. Research note N15. The household form in stage 8 waits for the image.
