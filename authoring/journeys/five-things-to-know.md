The content model listed the fourth thing as "where things are saved". Written that way it would break DEC-040, so it becomes "saving and copies": what the reader sees and does, not where data lives.

## Draft text

About 180 words. Everything in bold is a label to confirm against the app.

> **Five things to know**
>
> **1. A workspace is one family tree.** You can have more than one. **Brontë family of Haworth** is a sample workspace to practise in. Your own tree goes in a workspace you make.
>
> **2. People and families.** Add a person for each relative. A family joins a couple and their children. Link people into families, and uFTi draws the tree for you.
>
> **3. Facts and sources.** A fact is something you record, such as a birth with its date. A source is where you found it: a certificate, a census page, or what someone told you. Add a source to each fact. Then you will always know why you believe it.
>
> **4. Saving and copies.** uFTi saves your work as you go. To keep it safe, make a copy: export a family tree file and keep it somewhere else. Do it again after a big session.
>
> **5. Getting help.** Choose **Help** on any screen for the page about it. On the manual's home page, **I want to** lists common tasks. Or search the manual in the words you would use.

## To confirm

- That the app saves as you go, with no Save step for the workspace. If not, thing 4 changes to say how to save.
- That every screen has a Help control (Q22). If not, thing 5 points to the manual's search and the task finder only.
- The labels in bold.

## How it is tested

In the [novice sessions](novice-session-plan.md), the participant reads the draft aloud at the end of the Quick Start. The facilitator asks them to explain two of the five things back in their own words, and records the words they use.
