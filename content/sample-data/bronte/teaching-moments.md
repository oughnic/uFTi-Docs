# Teaching moments and open gaps

What we found while building the specification that the manual and the good practice guide can teach with, and what could not be verified. Each moment names its evidence so that a topic can cite it.

## Teaching moments found

| # | Moment | Where it shows in the dataset | Which topic can use it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One person, many spellings.** Patrick's name is Brunty in the parish register, Prunty in two references, Branty in the college list and Brontë in his own hand | T01; P01 name variants | Names and their variants; sound-alike search (stages 2 and 9) |
| 2 | **A married name hides a death.** Charlotte's death is indexed under Nicholls, not Brontë | NS1 | Recording where you looked; searching indexes |
| 3 | **Indexes vary the spelling.** Elizabeth Branwell is indexed as Bramwell | NS6 | Sound-alike search; why an index is a derivative source |
| 4 | **The 1841 census rounds ages down.** A man of 64 is written as 60, by rule, so the census disagrees with the baptism by design | T05 | Resolving conflicts: an age rounded down in 1841 |
| 5 | **Two records, one informant.** The burial register and the first memorial tablet give the same wrong ages for Branwell and Emily. Agreement between them is not independent confirmation | T03 | Analysing and correlating evidence |
| 6 | **"In the 39th year of her age" means 38.** The tablets use the older way of stating an age | C013, C072 | Reading old records; ages and approximations |
| 7 | **Three ages on three stones.** Anne's first tablet says 27, her gravestone 28, the 1858 tablet 29. The baptism settles it | T03 | Reconcile evidence; writing down a conclusion |
| 8 | **A biographer who did not check.** Gaskell admits a reviewer caught the age error and that she did not examine the register | T03; S01 footnote 1 | Why original records matter; why not to copy an authored work unchecked |
| 9 | **A reference work corrects itself.** The alumni database prints "Thornton-le-Dale" and corrects it to Thornton in an addendum | D10 | Judging reliability; reading a whole entry, including its notes |
| 10 | **Two reputable works, one digit apart.** The DNB gives Charlotte's wedding as 19 June, Gaskell as 29 June | D02 | Resolving conflicts: a transcription or printing error |
| 11 | **Check the weekday.** The Guiseley plaque says "Monday 29th Dec. 1812"; the calendar says that day was a Tuesday | D01 | Sanity checks on dates |
| 12 | **A date range is honest.** Maria's, Elizabeth's and Anne's birth years are bounded, not known. The dataset records ranges with their threads | T04 | Dates and uncertainty; date quality fields |
| 13 | **A baptism is a ceiling, not a birthday.** Elizabeth's baptism in August 1815 allows a birth in 1814 or 1815 | T04 | Dates and uncertainty |
| 14 | **A will is evidence of family.** Charlotte's will names her husband and provides for a child that never came; the probate act names the executor | C073, C074; privacy check | Sources and evidence: a probate record |
| 15 | **A transcription covers only some years.** FreeREG has Haworth burials for 1848 but not 1855 or 1861. Absence from it proves nothing about the register | NS2, NS4 | Negative evidence; recording where you looked |
| 16 | **The same place, different levels.** Patrick's birthplace is a townland (Emdale), a parish (Ahaderg or Drumballyroney) or a county, depending on the source | T02 | Places and their hierarchy |
| 17 | **Places that changed county or country.** Haworth, Thornton and Hartshead appear in every record as the West Riding of Yorkshire, and Haworth's modern postcode district is Bradford's; Banagher is "King's County" in Shorter (1896) and "County Offaly" in 2024, and the placenames database records the change of name (S28); County Down is now in Northern Ireland | Places in events.md; S07, S24, S28 | Places and boundaries that change. A topic must source the English changes before stating them; see the gaps below |
| 18 | **A posthumous publication.** *The Professor* appeared in 1857, two years after Charlotte died | E067 | Custom events; events after death |
| 19 | **Pen names keep the initials.** Currer, Ellis and Acton Bell | C062 | Name types: a pen name is a name, not a nickname |
| 20 | **The office has several names.** Patrick is "Minister", "Incumbent", "perpetual curate" and "V." (vicar) in different records for the same post | E013 | Occupations: record the words the source uses |
| 21 | **A site that needs a named applicant.** Irish civil records can be searched only after the searcher makes an application in their own name | Sources not opened | An authoring note, not a reader topic: some searches must be done by a person |
| 22 | **Two people, one name.** Maria the mother and Maria the daughter | P02, P03 | Duplicates: same name, same person? |
| 23 | **A file holds conclusions; research is the process.** Each fact in the sourced sample shows the value we conclude. The route to it (which sources said what, where they disagreed, how they were weighed) is in the citations, the register and the threads | The sourced sample; T01 to T05; D01 to D12 | Analysing and concluding; reconcile evidence; GEDCOM and its limits |
| 24 | **Some records are made during the event and some well after it.** This is the owner's observation: a birth is often registered some time after it, while a marriage is entered as part of the marriage. Our data shows the gap: Charlotte was baptised about ten weeks after her birth, and Nicholls's death in 1906 was registered 29 days after it. The general rule needs an authority before a topic states it. The app's default treats a civil birth registration as contemporary, primary evidence of the birth, with the registration standing in for the event (DEC-118) | C030, C031, C147; S06, S35 | Sources and evidence: how soon after the event a record was made, and who gave the information |

## Sources that refused access

*Encyclopaedia Britannica*, the Morgan Library catalogue, the *Dictionary of Irish Biography* and the West Yorkshire Archive Service website refused automated requests on 16 October 2026. A person can open them in a browser. None was needed for a claim in the register, but each could add an independent confirmation.
