# Novice sessions: the plan

Part of [WP0](README.md). Status: draft, 16 October 2026. This plan is written so one person can run the sessions from it alone. It tests the seven [journeys](README.md) and the Quick Start outlines for [Android phone](quick-start-android-phone.md) and [Windows](quick-start-windows.md) with people who have never used uFTi.

## 1. Purpose and measure

**Purpose.** Find out three things. Can a person who has never used uFTi finish the Quick Start alone in ten minutes? Do the journeys describe what people try to do? Which words do people use?

**The measure.** Four out of five novices finish the Quick Start unaided in ten minutes or less. That is success criterion 8 in the [plan](../authoring-plan.md#15-success-criteria).

**Second measures.**

- Four of five "I want to" questions reach the right entry in the task finder.
- Every page of the Quick Start is finished unaided by at least three participants.
- The words participants use are recorded for the glossary and the plain-language audit of labels (WP4).

## 2. Who to invite

Three to five people. Nobody who has used uFTi, helped build it or read this repository.

| Aim for | Why |
|---|---|
| At least one person who has never done family history | The newcomer, [Journey 1](journey-1-starting-from-nothing.md) |
| At least one young person, 11 to 14, with a parent or guardian present | [Journey 5](journey-5-a-young-persons-project.md), and the reading level |
| At least one person with a tree in another program or website | The switcher, [Journey 2](journey-2-bringing-a-tree-across.md) |
| At least one on an Android phone and one on Windows | The two wave-1 devices |
| If you can, one person who uses large text or a screen reader | The assistive technology audience |

Do not pay with anything that depends on the result. A thank-you gift is fine.

## 3. Before the sessions

Check each item.

- [ ] A release build of uFTi on an Android phone and on a Windows PC. Note the app version and the operating system version.
- [ ] If the app is in a store, leave it **not installed** so page 1 can be tested. If it is not in a store yet, install it first and start the clock at page 2. Write down which.
- [ ] The Brontë sample. Use the bundled sample if the app has one (Q19). If not, use the reference workspace from WP4b if it exists. If neither exists, make a small test workspace by hand. Use the ten people named in the [dataset strategy, section 3](../dataset-strategy.md#3-scope), names only. Add facts only where the evidence register has them.
- [ ] A family tree file exported from the sample, for the [Journey 2](journey-2-bringing-a-tree-across.md) task. Put it where the participant would find a downloaded file: the phone's Downloads, or the PC's Downloads folder.
- [ ] One printed card for the [Journey 3](journey-3-recording-a-find.md) task. It says: "Charlotte Brontë married Arthur Bell Nicholls."
- [ ] One printed public-domain picture from the dataset's media list, for the [Journey 5](journey-5-a-young-persons-project.md) and [Journey 7](journey-7-getting-a-photo-in.md) tasks. Record its source and licence.
- [ ] The seven Quick Start pages for the device, printed one per sheet, from the outline's **Steps** sections. Number them.
- [ ] The "Five things to know" draft, printed.
- [ ] The task finder list, printed, with the numbers hidden.
- [ ] A recording sheet per participant ([section 9](#9-the-recording-sheet)).
- [ ] A stopwatch. A pen. The consent text ([section 10](#10-consent-and-care)).
- [ ] A quiet room, or a quiet video call for a Windows session with the screen shared.

Run the whole session once yourself with a friend before the first real one.

## 4. The session, step by step

About 45 minutes. Keep to the times.

| Part | Minutes | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Welcome | 5 | Read the consent text. Say we are testing the app and the pages, not them. Agree a participant code. |
| Background | 3 | Three questions ([section 5](#5-background-questions)). |
| A. Quick Start | up to 15 | Hand over page 1. Start the clock. Say: "Work through these pages. Talk out loud as you go. I will not help unless you are stuck." Stop at 15 minutes even if unfinished. |
| B. Journey tasks | 15 | Two tasks chosen for this person ([section 6](#6-the-journey-tasks)). |
| C. Words and the task finder | 5 | Five "I want to" questions and the words test ([section 7](#7-words-and-the-task-finder)). |
| D. Five things to know | 3 | They read the draft aloud. They explain two things back. |
| Close | 3 | Three closing questions. Thank them. Delete their workspace in front of them. |

## 5. Background questions

1. "Have you ever made a family tree, on paper or in a program? Tell me about it."
2. "What do you use this phone or computer for most?"
3. "What would you call a family tree app's file, if you had to send one to someone?"

Write the answers down in their words.

## 6. The journey tasks

Give two tasks per person. Pick the ones that match their background, and make sure every journey is tried at least twice across all the sessions. Read each task aloud and hand over any card or picture it needs. Do not show the journey document.

| Journey | The task, read aloud | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| [1](journey-1-starting-from-nothing.md) | "Start a tree with you, your parents and one set of grandparents. You can use made-up names. Then show me the tree." | Nothing |
| [2](journey-2-bringing-a-tree-across.md) | "There is a family tree file from another program in your Downloads. Get it into uFTi and find Charlotte Brontë." | The exported file |
| [3](journey-3-recording-a-find.md) | "Here is a card. Record what it says on Charlotte's page, and say the card is where you found it." | The printed card |
| [4](journey-4-checking-a-doubt.md) | "In the sample, find the research note about an age in the 1841 census. Tell me, in your own words, what the two records say and what the note concludes." If the sample has no research note yet: "Here are two cards that disagree about a birth year. Record both, and write a note saying what you think." | The sample at stage 7, or two cards |
| [5](journey-5-a-young-persons-project.md) | "Imagine this is for school. Start a tree for a made-up family of six, where one child has two sets of parents. Add this picture to someone. Then tell me what you would check before showing it in class." | The printed picture |
| [6](journey-6-sharing-with-family.md) | "Make a copy of the sample tree and get it to me by email or a shared folder. Then tell me what is in the copy and what is not." | An address or folder to send to |
| [7](journey-7-getting-a-photo-in.md) | "Here is a printed picture. Get it into the sample and put it with the right person. Say what it is." | The printed picture |

A task ends when the participant says they are done, when they give up, or at eight minutes.

## 7. Words and the task finder

**The task finder.** Show the printed list. Read five of these aloud, one at a time, and ask: "Which line would you choose?" Record the number they point to.

1. "You have a photo of your grandmother. You want it in the app."
2. "Your tree is in another program. You want it here."
3. "You know your great-grandfather was born around 1890, but not the exact date."
4. "You have a certificate and want to record where a fact came from."
5. "You want a copy of your tree in case something happens to your phone."
6. "Your cousin wants your tree."
7. "You added someone by mistake."

**The words test.** Point at each word on the printed sheet and ask: "What do you think this means here?" Then: "What word would you have used?"

- Workspace
- Submitter
- Source
- Citation
- Repository
- Event
- Export

Record their answer in their words. Do not correct them.

## 8. Think-aloud prompts and the hint ladder

**Prompts.** Use these and nothing else while they work.

- "What are you looking for now?"
- "What do you expect to happen?"
- "What would you call that?"
- "Keep talking. What are you thinking?"

**The hint ladder.** Wait two minutes of being stuck, or until they ask for help. Then go one rung at a time.

| Rung | What you say or do | Counts as help? |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | A prompt from the list | No |
| 1 | "What does the page say to do next?" | No |
| 2 | Point to the step on the page | Yes |
| 3 | Point to the part of the screen | Yes |
| 4 | Do it for them | Yes. The page is failed. |

A page is **unaided** if it needed nothing above rung 1. It is **assisted** at rung 2 or 3. It is **failed** at rung 4, or if the participant gives up, or if the clock runs out.

## 10. Consent and care

Read this aloud and give them a copy.

> We are testing a family tree app and its first pages. We are not testing you. You can stop at any time, and you do not have to say why. We write notes, and we record you only if you say yes. We use a code, not your name. You do not need to type in real details about your family. Made-up names are fine. Anything you do type is deleted at the end, while you watch. The notes are kept until the findings are written up, then deleted.

- **Young people.** A parent or guardian agrees first and stays in the room. Keep the session to 30 minutes. Stop if they have had enough.
- **Real family details.** Say at the start that made-up names are fine. If they type real details, delete the workspace at the end in front of them. Never photograph or record the screen while real details are on it.
- **Hard subjects.** A family tree can bring up a death or a loss. If it does, stop the task and let them choose whether to go on.
- **Their device.** Prefer our test device. If they must use their own, install nothing without asking, and remove the app and its data afterwards if they wish.

## 11. Scoring the measure

| Participants | Pass |
|---|---|
| 5 | 4 or more finish unaided in ten minutes |
| 4 | 4 finish unaided in ten minutes |
| 3 | 3 finish unaided in ten minutes, and the result is provisional until two more sessions are run |

A participant who started at page 2 is still scored over ten minutes. Say so in the write-up, because installing is part of the real Quick Start.

If the measure is not met, the Quick Start outline changes and the sessions run again with new participants. Nothing is written in DITA until it passes.

## 12. Writing up

Within a week of the last session, write `findings-YYYY-MM-DD.md` in this folder, with these sections.

1. **Who took part.** A table of codes, background, device and app version. No names.
2. **The measure.** How many finished unaided in ten minutes, and the time for each.
3. **Page by page.** For each Quick Start page: how many unaided, assisted and failed, and the main reason for each failure.
4. **Journey by journey.** Whether the steps matched what people tried to do. Steps people took that the journey does not have. Questions people asked that the journey does not have.
5. **Words.** The words people used for each of the seven terms, and for the things they searched for.
6. **The task finder.** Which entries were found, which were missed, and the rewording proposed.
7. **For the app team.** Every tier 0 finding, in plain words, with how many participants hit it.
8. **Changes made.** The edits to the journeys, the Quick Start outlines, the task finder and the help-target list, each in its own pull request.
9. **Decisions to record.** Anything that becomes a decision goes in the [decision log](../decision-log.md).

The findings are the evidence for accepting WP0 ([plan, section 8](../authoring-plan.md#8-delivery-plan)).
